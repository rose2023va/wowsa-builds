# Customer Service Email Response Guidelines

Research-backed guidelines for drafting responses to customer email inquiries. This document is the basis for a Claude Code skill — review and edit before the skill gets built.

## Your requirements (locked in)

- No em-dashes, ever
- Concise
- No cheesy or unnecessary introductions
- Direct, to the point
- Easy to understand
- Polite, customer service tone

## Structure

1. **Subject line** — 4-5 words summarizing the reason for the email. If replying to an existing thread, never edit the subject line, it breaks threading.
2. **Greeting** — customer's name, nothing more elaborate.
3. **Open with purpose, not throat-clearing** — skip "I hope this email finds you well." It's been called "the cockroach of email openers, indestructible, omnipresent, and curiously devoid of personality." It wastes the preview line, the part of the email visible before opening, on words that say nothing. Lead with the answer or the point instead.
4. **Acknowledge the issue in one sentence** — show you read and understood what they asked, especially if they're frustrated. This is what separates a good response from a form-letter response. One sentence, not a paragraph.
5. **Give the answer or solution** — the actual point of the email. Steps if it's a process, a direct answer if it's a question.
6. **Close with a clear next step or invitation to follow up** — what happens now, or how to reach back out. No sign-off filler beyond a normal close.

## Tone rules

- Polite and professional, not stiff. Contractions are fine ("I'll," "we're").
- Use "I" rather than "we" when possible, it reads as a person answering, not a department.
- Match the customer's tone within reason, but never match anger or rudeness back.
- Never write in all caps, it reads as shouting regardless of intent.
- No slang, no forced casualness.

## Conciseness rules

- Cut filler phrases: "basically," "in order to," "just," "what I mean is," "absolutely."
- Cut weak modifiers: "really," "very," "quite," "extremely." They add length, not meaning.
- Use active voice. It's shorter and clearer than passive by default.
- Lead with the most important information. Context, if needed, comes after, briefly.
- One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a comma to hold two separate points, split it.
- No restating the customer's entire question back to them. They know what they asked.

## Formatting rules

- **No em-dashes.** Use a period, comma, or two sentences instead.
- Short paragraphs, 1-3 sentences. Big blocks of text don't get read.
- Numbered or bulleted steps for anything with more than one action.
- No unnecessary bolding, exclamation points, or emoji unless the brand voice explicitly calls for it.

## What to avoid entirely

- "I hope this email finds you well" and equivalents ("Thank you for your email" as a standalone opener with nothing else)
- Corporate hedge language: "We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused" as a stock line with no specifics
- Over-apologizing when nothing went wrong
- Jargon or internal terminology the customer wouldn't know
- Making promises that can't be kept ("this will never happen again")
- Sending the entire email thread back when only one detail (like a missed attachment) needs pointing out

## Handling specific situations

- **Simple question:** answer directly in the first line, then close.
- **Complaint or frustration:** one sentence acknowledging the specific issue (not a generic "we're sorry"), then the resolution or next step, then close. No over-apologizing.
- **Company's mistake:** acknowledge it plainly, say what's being done, no excessive apology.
- **Complex issue that can't be resolved immediately:** say plainly that it's being worked on, give a real timeframe if possible, and say when they'll hear back next. Don't leave it open-ended.
- **Can't fulfill the request:** say what's not possible and immediately follow with what is possible, if anything. Don't just close the door.

## Example skeleton

```
Subject: [4-5 words, specific to the issue]

Hi [Name],

[Direct answer or acknowledgment, one sentence]

[Explanation or steps, as short as the issue allows]

[Next step or how to follow up]

[Sign-off]
```
